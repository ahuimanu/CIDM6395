The Proposal Cycle

The proposal cycle is the written procedure that replaces a design meeting. You commit a proposal, I return a decision and numbered questions, you answer them, and the decision becomes final. Every step leaves a file in your repository.

The steps

StepWho actsWhat they doThe fileWhen
1YouCommit the proposal and your drawing of the project.proposal/proposal.md, proposal/diagram.png or .svgBy the due date posted in WTClass
2IReview the proposal and commit a decision with numbered questions.feedback/proposal.mdBy the review date posted in WTClass
3YouAnswer each question by number. You may add a recording of up to five minutes, linked from the file.proposal/consultation-response.mdBy the response date posted in WTClass
4IRead the answers. Settle the decision, or add follow-up questions.feedback/proposal.md (updated)After your response
5YouBuild.The prototype folderAfter a greenlight or a conditional greenlight

The four decisions

  1. Greenlight. The proposal is specific and feasible, and it meets the requirements. Build.
  2. Conditional greenlight. The direction is approved. Answer the numbered questions before major build work. The decision becomes a greenlight when the answers settle the conditions.
  3. Revise before greenlight. The idea has promise, but one or more requirements are not clear enough to approve. Revise proposal/proposal.md and answer the questions. I review the revision.
  4. Not evaluable. The proposal is missing, inaccessible, or too incomplete to judge.

Writing the response

Answer each question under its own number, in the order asked. Answer the question that was asked, with the specific fact it asks for: a file path, a source, a record count, a decision. If an answer changes the proposal, say so in the answer and update proposal/proposal.md to match.

The optional recording is for an answer that is easier to show than to write, such as a data source opened in a browser or a first script running. Keep it under five minutes and link it from the answer it supports.

Why it is written

A meeting leaves no record that both parties can check later. A written cycle does. At the end of the term, the proposal, the questions, and your answers together state what was agreed, and the final review compares the delivered work with that record. Tool approvals are recorded the same way: any tool other than Python is approved or declined in feedback/proposal.md.

Dates, submission steps, and scoring are on the WTClass pages. The proposal instructions are on the WTClass assignment page; what the proposal must show is in the proposal guide.
