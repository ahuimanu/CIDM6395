Data Mining and Management (DM)

Data Mining and Management makes data trustworthy and usable. In the capstone it is the area that turns raw sources into data the rest of the system can rely on.

What DM does in a working system

DM extracts data from its sources, cleans and transforms it, stores it in a designed structure, and mines it for patterns. Its output is data that the rest of the system can trust: a schema with known meaning, a pipeline that runs again, a table where one record means one thing.

Most of the real work in a capstone with real data is DM work. Two sources that disagree on a name, a field that changed definition between years, duplicate records, late corrections: each is a DM problem, and each is part of the evidence that the data are real.

Where DM meets the other areas

In Figure 01, DM overlaps each of the other three areas, and each overlap is a recognizable practice:

  • DM with BA: the data pipeline. Managed data prepared for analysis.
  • DM with SS: the persistence layer. Software built over managed data.
  • DM with CN: data protection and governance. Data that is protected, governed, and kept within its terms of use.

DM in the prototype

In a prototype, DM is usually the step between the source and the analysis. Its test in the proposal: remove DM, and the analysis runs on data no one has checked. Show DM in the repository as a schema, a pipeline that runs from the README, and a record of the cleaning and matching decisions you made. The data constraints on the prototype guide are, in large part, DM requirements.
