Data Mining and Management (DM)

Data Mining and Management makes data usable and trustworthy. In a capstone built on real data, much of the work is DM work.

From raw sources to tables you can trust

DM extracts data from its sources, cleans and transforms it, stores it in a designed structure, and mines it for patterns. The rest of the system depends on the result: a schema in which each field has one meaning, and a pipeline that produces the same tables each time it runs.

Real data bring problems of their own. Two sources may disagree about a company's name, a field may change its definition between years, and records may arrive twice, late, or corrected. In the example project on this site, matching the company names in the complaint data to the lender identifiers in the loan data is the step that takes the most judgment, and it is DM work. Problems of this kind are evidence that the data are real, which is one reason a pre-cleaned sample dataset makes a weak capstone.

The courses behind it

CourseWhat its objectives emphasize
CIDM 6350Database design: entity-relationship modeling, relational schemas, normalization, SQL for definition and retrieval, and a database application built for an organization's requirements.
CIDM 6351Extraction, transformation, and loading: warehouses, lakes, marts, and pipelines, web scraping, and preparing high-quality data for analysis.
CIDM 6355Data mining concepts and algorithms, problem formulation, data preparation, supervised and unsupervised techniques, model evaluation, and responsible use of AI tools.

The boundaries between areas are not sharp here. CIDM 6355 covers model evaluation and the communication of results, which is BA work as well, and CIDM 6303, counted toward SS, teaches the SQL and normalization that form the core of CIDM 6350.

What to show in your repository

In the proposal, say what the analysis would lose without DM; in the example project, it would run on complaint records that no one had matched to a lender or checked. In the repository, show the schema, a pipeline that runs from the README, and a record of the cleaning and matching decisions you made. Much of what the prototype guide requires of your data is DM work.
