Networking and Cybersecurity (CN)

Networking and Cybersecurity covers the networks a system runs on and the controls that protect it. In the capstone, CN is the area that makes a prototype safe to run on real data.

Controls along the whole flow

CN deals with access, credentials, encryption, integrity, risk, and policy. Its contribution to a prototype is the reviewer's confidence that the data were not altered, that no secrets were exposed, and that the system respects the terms under which the data were released.

CN seldom acts at a single point in the flow. Its controls apply at every step, which is why the chain view in Figure 02 draws CN as a band beneath the other areas. In Figure 01, CN meets SS in secure delivery, DM in data protection and governance, and BA in security analytics.

Two of these courses cross area lines. CIDM 4380 builds and deploys systems, which makes it as much SS as CN, and CIDM 6356 analyzes data to detect fraud, which makes it as much BA.

What to show in your repository

The proposal review watches for one risk in particular, a project that leaves CN for the paper. A prototype that runs on real data nearly always has something to protect, such as an API key, a file whose integrity matters, or a field whose use the data's terms restrict, and the proposal should say what CN protects in yours. In the repository, show credentials kept out of the code, integrity checks, access rules, and a record of the data's terms of use. If you want to work with security data itself, CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is a good source to start from.
