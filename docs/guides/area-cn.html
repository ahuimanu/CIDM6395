Networking and Cybersecurity (CN)

Networking and Cybersecurity governs how data and software move and who may use them. In the capstone it is the area that makes a prototype safe to run on real data.

What CN does in a working system

CN covers the networks a system runs on and the controls that protect it: access, credentials, encryption, integrity, risk assessment, and policy. Its output is trust. A reviewer can believe that the data were not altered, that secrets are not exposed, and that the system respects the terms under which the data were released.

CN often acts along the whole flow, not at one point in it. In the chain view of Figure 02 it is drawn as a band under the other areas for that reason.

Where CN meets the other areas

In Figure 01, CN overlaps each of the other three areas, and each overlap is a recognizable practice:

  • CN with SS: secure delivery. Software that is secured and deployed safely.
  • CN with DM: data protection and governance. Data that is protected, governed, and kept within its terms of use.
  • CN with BA: security analytics. Analysis of security data, or analysis under a privacy constraint.

CN in the prototype

In a prototype, CN is often the area students leave for the paper. That is a mistake, because a prototype on real data always has something to protect: an API key, a file whose integrity matters, a field the data's terms restrict. Its test in the proposal: remove CN, and something unsafe happens. Show CN in the repository as credentials kept out of the code, integrity checks, access rules, and a record of the data's terms of use. For a project built on security data, the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog from CISA is a good source.
