Networking and Cybersecurity (CN)

Networking and Cybersecurity covers the networks a system runs on and the controls that protect it. In the capstone it is what makes a prototype safe to run on real data.

In a working system

CN deals with access, credentials, encryption, integrity, risk, and policy. What it contributes is trust: a reviewer can believe the data were not altered, the secrets were not exposed, and the system respects the terms under which the data were released.

CN rarely sits at one point in the flow. It applies at every step, which is why the chain view in Figure 02 draws it as a band under the other areas. In Figure 01, CN meets SS in secure delivery, DM in data protection and governance, and BA in security analytics.

Two of these courses cross area lines. CIDM 4380 builds and deploys systems, which is as much SS as CN, and CIDM 6356 analyzes data to detect fraud, which is as much BA.

In your prototype

Students often leave CN for the paper. A prototype on real data nearly always has something to protect, though: an API key, a file whose integrity matters, a field the data's terms restrict. Take CN away and something unsafe happens. In the repository, show credentials kept out of the code, integrity checks, access rules, and a record of the data's terms of use. If you want to build on security data itself, CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is a good place to start.
