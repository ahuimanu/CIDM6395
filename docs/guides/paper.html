The synthesis paper

The synthesis paper argues, in five to ten pages, that your prototype integrates the four areas, and then projects the system it demonstrates one year forward.

The argument

The paper makes one claim: the prototype shows that you can integrate the four areas, and the full project is feasible. It gets there by defining each area in your own words, explaining how the areas work together in your project (what each one receives, what it does, and what it passes on), supporting each point with your appraisals and the artifacts in your portfolio, and arguing why the prototype counts as a synthesis. It ends with the projection described below.

Defining the areas

Define each area by what it does in a working system you have built, not by what a catalog says a course covers. "DM is the discipline of storing and retrieving data" is a catalog line. "In my project, DM turns two sources that disagree about a lender's name into one table the analysis can trust" is a definition from practice.

The prototype integrates SS and at least two other areas, but the paper covers all four. If the prototype leaves an area out, say how the full project needs it and what it would do there. This is the center of Figure 01: the paper accounts for the whole space even when the prototype sits in one part of it.

The one-year projection

The last part of the paper projects the system forward one year, to what the full project would be after a year of full-time, competent work. The dashed boxes in your proposal drawing give you its outline. For each, state what it adds, which area does the work, and what the prototype has already shown that makes it feasible. Where a step in the projection does not build on something the prototype already does, say so plainly; that is a risk, and the reader should see it as one.

What it is not

The paper is not the four appraisals rewritten. The appraisals assess you one area at a time, while the paper argues about a project across all of them; it cites the appraisals without summarizing them. Nor is it a manual for the prototype. The README covers how to run it.

Length

Five to ten pages. Under five rarely leaves room for the definitions, the integration, and the projection together, and over ten usually means the paper is describing parts rather than connecting them. The stack and hub views in Figure 02 can help organize the middle of the paper. Put detail in the portfolio and cite it.
