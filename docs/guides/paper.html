Guide · The synthesis paper

The synthesis paper

The synthesis paper argues that your prototype integrates the four areas of the program, and it then projects the system the prototype demonstrates one year forward, to the full project. It runs five to ten pages.

The argument

The paper makes a single claim, that the prototype shows you can integrate the four areas and that the full project is feasible, and every part of the paper serves that claim. It defines each area in your own words. It explains how the areas work together in your project, stating for each one what it receives, what it does, and what it passes on. It supports each point with your appraisals and the artifacts in your portfolio, and it argues why the prototype counts as a synthesis. It ends with the projection described below.

Defining the areas

Define each area by what it does in a system you have built, rather than by what a catalog says a course covers, because the paper has to show how you understand each area. "DM is the discipline of storing and retrieving data" is a catalog line. "In my project, DM turns two sources that disagree about a lender's name into one table the analysis can trust" is a definition drawn from practice.

The prototype integrates SS and at least two other areas, but the paper covers all four. If the prototype leaves an area out, state how the full project needs that area and what it would do there. In terms of Figure 01, the prototype may sit in a three-way overlap, while the paper accounts for the center, where all four areas meet.

The one-year projection

The final part of the paper projects the system forward one year, to what the full project would be after a year of full-time, competent work. The dashed boxes in your proposal drawing give the outline. For each one, state what it adds, which area does the work, and what the prototype has already shown that makes it feasible. Where a step in the projection does not build on something the prototype already does, say so, and treat that step as a risk in the paper.

Its relation to the other deliverables

The appraisals assess you one area at a time, while the paper argues about a project across all four, so the paper cites the appraisals as evidence and does not summarize them. How to run the prototype belongs in its README, which the paper can refer to.

Length

The paper runs five to ten pages. Within that range it has to hold the definitions, the account of how the areas work together, and the projection, so put supporting detail in the portfolio and cite it. The stack and hub views in Figure 02 can help organize the middle of the paper.
