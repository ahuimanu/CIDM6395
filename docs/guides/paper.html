The Synthesis Paper

The synthesis paper is a five-page argument that your prototype is a feasible synthesis of the four areas. It is written from the center of the interaction space: it accounts for all four areas, including any the prototype does not integrate. This guide explains what the paper must argue and what it is not.

What the paper argues

The paper makes one claim: the prototype shows that you can integrate the four areas, and the full project is feasible. Everything in the paper supports that claim. The paper does four things in order:

  1. It defines each of the four areas in your own words.
  2. It explains how the areas work together in your project, as a process: what each area receives, what it does, and what it passes on.
  3. It supports each claim with your evolution appraisals and the artifacts in your portfolio.
  4. It argues why the prototype is a feasible synthesis, and what the full project would add.

Defining the areas

A definition in your own words is not a catalog description. The catalog says what a course covers. Your definition says what the area does in a working system, as you have practiced it. "DM is the discipline of storing and retrieving data" is a catalog line. "In my project, DM turns two sources that do not agree on a lender's name into one table that the analysis can trust" is a definition grounded in practice.

All four areas, even if the prototype has three

The prototype integrates SS and at least two other areas. The paper accounts for all four. If the prototype leaves one area out, the paper states how the full project needs it and what it would do there. The center of Figure 01 is where this happens: the paper is written from the place where all four areas meet, even when the prototype sits in a three-way overlap.

What the paper is not

The paper is not a rewrite of the four appraisals. The appraisals assess you, area by area. The paper argues about a project, across the areas. It cites the appraisals as evidence. It does not summarize them.

The paper is not a user manual for the prototype either. The README explains how to run the prototype. The paper explains why its design is a synthesis.

Five pages

The limit is five pages. A student may object that four areas, a prototype, and a forecast cannot fit. The limit is deliberate. A synthesis that needs fifteen pages to state has usually not been synthesized yet: the writer is still describing parts. Five pages forces the argument to say how the parts connect.
