The Presentation

The presentation is a video of 5 to 15 minutes with slides. It shows the produced artifact working, explains how the artifact integrates the areas, and forecasts what the full project would do. This guide explains what the presentation must show.

Show the artifact

The presentation is about the prototype you built, not the idea you had. A viewer should see the prototype run on real data, along the complete path of the first slice: the data arriving, the processing in each area, and the output a user sees. A presentation that describes the project without showing it running cannot show that the project works.

Explain the synthesis

As the prototype runs, connect what the viewer sees to the areas. When the data load, that is SS. When the names are matched to lender identifiers, that is DM. Each area should be visible in the artifact at the moment it acts. Figure 02 is a model: the chain view follows the order of the demonstration.

Forecast the full project

End with what the full project would do over about a year, and why it would work. Use the dashed boxes of your proposal drawing (Figure 03). The forecast is credible when the first slice has already shown each kind of work the full project needs at a smaller scale.

A suggested structure

PartContentApproximate share
The pretextWho has the problem and what they decideone minute
The designThe process flow and the first slice, from your drawingone to two minutes
The demonstrationThe prototype running along the complete path, with each area identified as it actsmost of the time
The forecastWhat the full project adds, and why it is feasibleone to two minutes

Length and form

The video runs at least 5 and at most 15 minutes. Submit the slides as PPTX or an equivalent, and the video as a file in the repository or a clearly labeled link that opens without a login. Under five minutes is rarely enough to show a complete path. Over fifteen usually means the presentation is describing parts instead of showing how they connect.
