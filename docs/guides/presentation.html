The Presentation

The presentation is the bridge between the other artifacts: the portfolio and bio, the paper, and the prototype. It is a video of 5 to 15 minutes with slides. It shows the prototype working, connects it to the competency your portfolio documents, and carries the paper's projection forward. This guide explains what the presentation must show.

A bridge between the artifacts

Each artifact answers one question. The portfolio and bio show what you can do. The prototype shows that the areas work together. The paper argues why it is a synthesis and where it goes in a year. The presentation is the one place a viewer sees all of them at once, so it connects them: this competency, from this portfolio, made this prototype possible, and the paper projects it this far. A presentation that shows only the prototype leaves the viewer to make those connections alone.

Show the artifact

The presentation is about the prototype you built, not the idea you had. A viewer should see the prototype run on real data, along its complete path: the data arriving, the processing in each area, and the output a user sees. A presentation that describes the project without showing it running cannot show that the project works.

Explain the synthesis

As the prototype runs, connect what the viewer sees to the areas. When the data load, that is SS. When the names are matched to lender identifiers, that is DM. Each area should be visible in the artifact at the moment it acts. Figure 02 is a model: the chain view follows the order of the demonstration.

Forecast the full project

End with what the full project would do over about a year, and why it would work. Use the dashed boxes of your proposal drawing (Figure 03). The forecast is credible when the synthesis prototype has already shown each kind of work the full project needs at a smaller scale.

Length and form

The video runs at least 5 and at most 15 minutes. Submit the slides as PPTX or an equivalent, and the video as a file in the repository or a clearly labeled link that opens without a login. Under five minutes is rarely enough to show a complete path. Over fifteen usually means the presentation is describing parts instead of showing how they connect.
