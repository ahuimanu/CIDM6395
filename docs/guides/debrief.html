The Debrief

The debrief is a 30-minute conversation with me after you deliver the final package. It is part oral defense, part feedback, and part exit interview. It is where you show that you understand what you built. This guide explains how it runs and how to prepare.

Why there is a debrief

An artifact alone is not the same as understanding. A repository can show what was built. It cannot show whether the person who submitted it can explain the path from intent to design to code to system. The debrief tests that, in conversation. It matters more now that AI tools can produce working code: "the model did that" is an acceptable answer only if you can then explain why you accepted what the model produced.

How it runs

TimeSegmentWhat happens
0 to 3 minOpeningWe confirm the project and the repository I reviewed.
3 to 8 minYour overviewYou summarize what you built, the problem it addresses, and where the strongest evidence is.
8 to 23 minFive questionsI ask questions grounded in specific files in your repository.
23 to 27 minReflectionYou identify your strongest learning and the biggest limitation you did not resolve.
27 to 30 minCloseI summarize the feedback and any follow-up.

The five questions come from five categories:

  1. Design rationale. Why you chose an approach, and what alternatives you rejected.
  2. Technical implementation. How a specific part of the prototype works.
  3. User need. What the result is worth to the person in your pretext.
  4. Evidence. How the paper and the portfolio support the prototype.
  5. Limitations, next steps, and AI use. What does not work yet, what comes next, and how you verified what AI tools produced.

The live demonstration

I may ask you to demonstrate the prototype live for at least five minutes, from the source data to the output. Have it ready to start from your own README. If something in your environment blocked the run during review (an API key, an account), the demonstration is where you show it working.

How to prepare

  1. Start the prototype from your README on a clean clone, the day before.
  2. Reread your proposal and identify each place the final work differs from it, and why.
  3. Pick the three files you would show a stranger first: one portfolio artifact, one prototype artifact, and one paper or presentation artifact.
  4. For each AI-assisted part of the work, be ready to say what the tool produced, what you changed, and how you checked it.

The debrief is not a quiz and not a second presentation. It is a professional conversation about your work, and the best preparation is to have done the work yourself.
