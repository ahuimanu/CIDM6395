The debrief

The debrief is a thirty-minute conversation with me after you deliver the final package. It serves as an oral defense of the work, as a time for feedback, and as an exit interview from the program.

Why we meet

Your repository shows what you built, but not whether you can explain how the work went from an idea to a running system, and the debrief is where you show that you can. The question applies with particular force to work done with AI tools: "the model wrote that part" is an acceptable answer only if you can then explain why you accepted what it wrote.

How it runs

MinutesWhat happens
0 to 3We confirm the project and the commit I reviewed.
3 to 8You summarize what you built, the problem it addresses, and where your strongest evidence is.
8 to 23I ask five questions, each tied to specific files in your repository.
23 to 27You tell me what you learned most and which limitation you did not resolve.
27 to 30I summarize the feedback and any follow-up.

The five questions cover your design choices and the alternatives you rejected, how a specific part of the prototype works, what the result is worth to the person in your pretext, how the paper and the portfolio support the prototype, and what does not work yet, including how you checked anything an AI tool produced.

The live demonstration

I may ask you to run the prototype for at least five minutes, from the source data to the output, so have it ready to start from your own README. If something blocked the run during my review, such as a personal API key, the demonstration is where you show the prototype working.

Preparing

  1. The day before, start the prototype from your README in a fresh clone.
  2. Reread your proposal and note each place where the final work differs from it, and why.
  3. Choose the three files you would show a stranger first: one from the portfolio, one from the prototype, and one from the paper or presentation.
  4. For each part of the work an AI tool helped with, be ready to say what it produced, what you changed, and how you checked it.

The debrief is scheduled in the final week. Specific details about dates, submission, and scoring are in WTClass.
