The debrief

After you deliver, we talk for thirty minutes about what you built. It is part oral defense, part feedback, and part exit interview.

Why we meet

A repository shows what was built. It cannot show whether the person who submitted it can explain how the work got from an idea to a running system, and the debrief is where you show that. AI tools make this matter more than it used to. "The model wrote that part" is an acceptable answer only if you can go on to explain why you accepted what it wrote.

How it runs

MinutesWhat happens
0 to 3We confirm the project and the commit I reviewed.
3 to 8You summarize what you built, the problem it addresses, and where your strongest evidence is.
8 to 23I ask five questions, each tied to specific files in your repository.
23 to 27You tell me what you learned most and which limitation you did not resolve.
27 to 30I summarize the feedback and any follow-up.

The five questions come from five areas of the work. One asks why you chose an approach and what you rejected. One asks how a specific part of the prototype works. One asks what the result is worth to the person in your pretext. One asks how the paper and the portfolio support the prototype. The last asks what does not work yet, what you would do next, and how you checked anything an AI tool produced.

The live demonstration

I may ask you to run the prototype for at least five minutes, from the source data to the output, so have it ready to start from your own README. If something blocked the run during my review, such as a personal API key, this is where you show it working.

Preparing

  1. The day before, start the prototype from your README in a fresh clone.
  2. Reread your proposal and note each place the final work differs from it, and why.
  3. Choose the three files you would show a stranger first: one from the portfolio, one from the prototype, and one from the paper or presentation.
  4. For each part of the work an AI tool helped with, be ready to say what it produced, what you changed, and how you checked it.

The debrief is scheduled in the final week. Specific details about dates, submission, and scoring are in WTClass.
