Three Views of One Project

This figure draws one project in three ways. The chain shows the order in which the areas act, the stack shows which parts depend on which, and the hub shows what each area contributes to the decision the project supports.

Grey labels are overlaps from Figure 01.

Reading the figure

The three views describe the same design, so you use all of them rather than choosing one. Each leaves out something the others make visible: the chain makes it easy to miss that CN acts at every step, the stack does not show the order in which the data arrive, and the hub does not show how the areas connect to one another.

This example integrates all four areas, although SS with any two of the others would meet the rule. SS appears twice in the chain because software both loads the data and delivers the result. I require SS because the prototype has to run, and the hub shows that the requirement does not make SS the center of the design; the center of this project is the examiner's decision.

Your proposal drawing uses the chain, since the proposal states the order in which the areas act, and Figure 03 shows how to mark the synthesis prototype on it. The stack and the hub are more useful in the paper.
